01 / Challenge

Manual hydraulic QC does not scale

Checking floodway and water-surface profiles manually requires repeated inspection of cross sections, hydraulic variables, and model outputs. Each stream adds another review cycle, making larger groups of models particularly time-consuming.

02 / Engineering approach

Read, validate, locate, report

I developed a Python application that reads HEC-RAS steady-flow HDF files, runs configurable checks, identifies cross sections that need engineering attention, and produces Excel and GIS shapefile outputs.

The application also extracts encroachment information for reporting and spatial review. Individual checks depend on available HDF profiles and variables.

04 / Impact

More than 300 models processed

The application has processed more than 300 hydraulic models. Batch mode allows engineers to submit multiple streams together, rather than launch QC separately for each model.

Using the full reported time ranges (180–300 minutes manually versus 1–3 minutes automated), the estimated processing-time reduction is 98.3–99.7%, equivalent to roughly 2 hours 57 minutes to 4 hours 59 minutes saved per stream. These figures compare automated execution with the previous manual screening process; engineering review of flagged issues remains necessary.

05 / Output and validation

Actionable Excel and spatial findings

Excel reports identify flagged observations and supporting values, while exported shapefiles help reviewers locate potentially problematic cross sections in GIS. The tool is a screening aid: findings require engineering interpretation and do not independently certify model compliance.
